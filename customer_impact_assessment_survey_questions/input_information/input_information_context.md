# Input information context

This file outlines context related to the input information for this work.

## Motivation

The brief came from company leadership: scientifically assess whether the product enables
customers to be more focused, feel more connected, get more done in less time, and achieve their
goals faster. The ask was for at most 10 questions that could be put to each user in a customer
organization about once a quarter, to test whether the product had genuinely improved on the
status quo. (Verbatim internal quotes have been removed for publication.)

Six starter questions were supplied with the brief:
1. I feel more focused (using Convictional over Slack) (likert scale)
2. I feel more connected with my team (...) (likert scale)
3. We are more likely to achieve our goals (...) (likert scale)
4. I'm able to make decisions faster than before (...) (likert scale)
5. My decisions are higher qualify (...) (likert scale)
6. It's easier to find what I need when I need it (...) (likert scale)

## Likert scale

The file likert_scale.png is a reference for the Likert scale, and what the scale means for different types of questions.

The different question types are: satisfaction, quality, frequency, performance, importance, and focus.

## Prior art from wellbeing survey instruments

Published workplace-wellbeing survey instruments were used as a source of inspiration for question
design — in particular the way they separate variables (work time, job conditions, subjective
wellbeing) from the measures used for each. The specific instrument consulted was a third-party
academic study's variables-and-measures table, which is not redistributed here.

## Goal of customer impact assessment survey questions ideation

We want to use the six starter questions and build on them to come up with survey questions we can use to assess customer impact of our product.

Maximum 10 questions.

Results from these surveys will be used to scientifically investigate if we enable customers to be more focused, feel more connected, get more done in less time, and achieve their goals faster.

The questions will be evaluated by customers using a Likert scale. The details of the scale depend on the type of question (satisfaction, quality, frequency, performance, importance, focus, etc).

Workplace-wellbeing survey instruments are a useful source of inspiration for new questions, since they measure something structurally similar to what we want to measure (in our case, business wellbeing rather than personal wellbeing).

The goal is to come up with a maximum of 10 questions we can ask each user within a customer organization to assess whether we have truly improved upon the status quo.

The 6 starter questions must be included in the final 10, but they can be tweaked if wording needs to be improved.
