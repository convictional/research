# Customer Impact Assessment Survey - Draft for Review

## 1 - Revisions to the initial questions

Leadership proposed 6 starting questions to assess whether Convictional improves customer outcomes. We refined each to address two systemic issues: **all 6 used comparative framing** ("more focused," "faster than before," "higher quality") and **inconsistent grammatical subjects** ("I feel" / "We are" / "It's").

Comparative framing is problematic for a quarterly survey because it requires respondents to accurately recall their state from months ago (recall bias), implies improvement is expected (leading), and produces scores that aren't comparable across quarters. Every validated workplace instrument (Gallup Q12, UWES, Edmondson) uses absolute framing instead - you track change by comparing scores across quarters statistically, not by asking people to estimate their own change.

| # | Original | Revised | Key changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | I feel more focused (using Convictional over Slack) | I am able to focus on my most important work without unnecessary distractions | Dropped Slack comparison (double-barreled). Absolute framing. |
| Q2 | I feel more connected with my team | I feel well-informed about my team's work, priorities, and decisions | Sharpened "connected" to what Decide provides: being informed. "Connected" is vague and could mean social bonding. |
| Q3 | We are more likely to achieve our goals | I have clear visibility into our goals and how we are progressing toward them | Switched "We" to "I." Measures the mechanism (visibility) rather than the outcome (achievement), which depends on too many factors outside the tool. |
| Q4 | I'm able to make decisions faster than before | When a decision needs to be made, I have the context I need to move forward quickly | Dropped "than before." Reframed from pure speed (which can reward recklessness) to having context to move quickly. |
| Q5 | My decisions are higher quality | I feel confident that important decisions are made with sufficient information and input | Biggest rework. Self-assessed decision quality has weak validity (Dunning-Kruger). Shifted to measuring the decision process instead. |
| Q6 | It's easier to find what I need when I need it | I can quickly find the information I need when I need it | Lightest touch. Switched comparative "easier" to absolute "quickly." Changed "It's" to "I" for consistency. |

All 6 revised questions now share: consistent "I" subject, absolute framing (trackable quarter-over-quarter), and direct ties to what Decide provides.

## 2 - New questions to fill identified gaps

The 6 revised questions collectively cover focus, team awareness, goal visibility, decision efficiency, decision process quality, and information retrieval. In aggregate, they also implicitly cover productivity ("get more done in less time") since that's the natural outcome when focus, decision speed, goal clarity, and information access all improve.

We identified three gaps where the existing questions do not capture something genuinely distinct:

**Q7 - Knowledge Persistence**
*"I can understand the context behind past work and discussions when I need to revisit them"*

Q6 measures whether you can *retrieve* information in the moment. Knowledge persistence is about whether that information *exists to be found* - whether the reasoning, discussion, and context behind past work is preserved over time. This is one of Decide's most concrete structural advantages: Posts record decisions with context, events create audit trails, workspaces persist. In Slack, that context scrolls away within weeks. This construct also compounds over time, making it a valuable signal for quarterly measurement.

**Q8 - Autonomy / Control Over Time**
*"I am able to engage with team discussions and updates on my own schedule"*

Q1 measures focus *during* work. Autonomy is about controlling *when* you engage with communication. You could be focused when working (Q1 high) but still feel compelled to check and respond constantly. This tests whether Decide's fundamental async-first thesis is landing: people should engage with discussions, decisions, and updates on their own schedule rather than in real-time. Published workplace-wellbeing instruments measure "schedule control" as a distinct variable, confirming this is a recognized construct.

**Q9 - Collaboration Quality**
*"I am able to collaborate effectively with my colleagues on shared work"*

Q2 measures being *informed* (passive - receiving information). Q5 measures whether decisions have the right *inputs* (structural). Collaboration quality is about the *active experience* of co-creation: is working together smooth and productive? You could be well-informed (Q2 high) and confident in decision inputs (Q5 high) but still find collaboration clunky. Decide enables this through real-time document editing, threaded discussions, collaborative email, and shared goal tracking.

## 3 - Likert scale assignment

We use two scale types, chosen based on the nature of each question:

**Frequency** (Never | Rarely | Sometimes | Often | Always) for questions about capabilities that recur and vary in consistency - focus, decision context, information retrieval, and schedule autonomy. These are experiences where the success rate matters: "How often is this true for you?" The midpoint "Sometimes" is concretely meaningful (roughly half the time), which is more informative than "Neither agree nor disagree" for fluctuating capabilities.

**Agreement** (Strongly disagree | Disagree | Neither agree nor disagree | Agree | Strongly agree) for questions about persistent states and general assessments - being informed, goal visibility, confidence in the decision process, knowledge persistence, and collaboration effectiveness. These are overall evaluations of ongoing conditions, not per-instance reports. Agreement captures the strength of that assessment.
