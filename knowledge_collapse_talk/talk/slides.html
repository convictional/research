I · Foundations · 03/24

Starting point

Knowledge and learning feed each other.

We learn by solving problems. When we do, we produce knowledge — for ourselves, and for everyone else working on similar problems.

In the paper, "learning" is just a formal name for problem-solving.

Convictional
I · Foundations · 04/24

Definitions

Knowledge comes in two flavours.

General · G

Shared, explicit, public.

Papers, blog posts, docs, Stack Overflow answers, the wiki, the Slack thread from last October that solved it for everyone.

Individually infinitesimal. Collectively the thing a field runs on.

Idiosyncratic · I

Private, tacit, yours.

Your intuition, your context, your taste. Motivations — safety, comfort, status, curiosity — and the judgment you've built from doing the work.

Doesn't fit in a doc. Shows up when you make a call no one else is watching.

Two examples

A doctor with, and without, access to the medical literature. A developer debugging an unknown language with no documentation.

Convictional
I · Foundations · 05/24

Where knowledge lives

Communities of practice — "islands."

An island is a community that shares context — a field, a company, a subreddit.

Each island has a common state: the general knowledge everyone on it can draw on.

Islands grow and shrink. They overlap at the edges. Some are vast. Some are fourteen people on a Tuesday.

II · The model · 07/24

Modelling move

Learning, as a prediction problem.

To make it tractable, the authors reframe learning as prediction.

An agent trying to solve something is an agent predicting two things:

  • the outcome of the common world state — how the field, market, or peers will respond
  • the outcome of their private state — what will work for them specifically

They update both iteratively as they work.

Each action produces
A private signal

Tacit knowledge that stays with the agent.

A public signal

Explicit knowledge that goes into the island's common state. The pump that keeps general knowledge flowing.

Convictional
II · The model · 08/24

Success

Getting both predictions right.

Four possible outcomes — common and private, each right or wrong.

  • (0, 0)  both wrong
  • (1, 0)  common right, private wrong
  • (0, 1)  common wrong, private right
  • (1, 1)  both right — the corner you want to live in

Payoff is a function f(G, I) over the four corners — the degree to which what actually happened aligns with what you predicted.

Convictional
II · The model · 09/24

Load-bearing

Two assumptions that do a lot of work.

01
Private effort only pays off when the common state is understood.
Payoff from your private cleverness scales with how much general knowledge you've already absorbed. With zero general knowledge, your private effort returns zero.
02
General knowledge and private learning compound each other.
Getting both predictions right is worth more than the sum of getting each right alone. Solid general knowledge amplifies the value of private effort — they're complements, not substitutes.
Flag this one

Is assumption 1 right? A doctor without medical literature can still stop the bleeding. We'll come back to both assumptions at the end.

Convictional
II · The model · 10/24

Cost of effort

Alpha controls how fast the cost of effort compounds.

c(e) = 1 α · eα

α is the curvature — how steeply cost rises as effort increases.

  • Low α  cost is nearly linear — each unit of effort costs about the same
  • High α  cost compounds steeply — the juice has to be worth the squeeze

Alpha comes back as the first collapse threshold — and what it implies might surprise you.

III · Dynamics · 12/24

The engine

The loop the authors call F.

Each period, the model runs the same loop.

F takes today's common knowledge Xt and returns tomorrow's Xt+1.

What matters is simple: does F give back more knowledge than it received, or less? Where it gives back exactly the same, the system has found a resting point.

Convictional
III · Dynamics · 13/24

The new tool

AI reshapes the effort ↔ precision trade.

Problem-solving, before

Intensive private effort → answer, with a public signal produced as a byproduct.

Problem-solving, after

AI recommendation → answer. No effort, no byproduct.

Humans have always adopted tools that improve precision-per-effort — writing, the internet, Stack Overflow. AI is the same move, further.

Convictional
III · Dynamics · 14/24

How AI enters the model

Three assumptions about AI.

01
AI doesn't improve general knowledge on its own. It generates no common signal by itself.
02
Using AI doesn't change the human's public signal. The human contributes the same amount to the common pool, whether or not AI was involved.
03
AI's ability to improve general knowledge doesn't scale with capability. Implicit in the model — better AI means more precise private answers, not more common contribution.

Number three is the one most worth poking at. We'll come back to it.

Convictional
III · Dynamics · 15/24

Headline results

Two things fall out of the machine.

Observation 1

General knowledge degrades as AI adoption increases.

Humans spend less effort on learning. The public signal flow slows as AI substitutes for the work that used to produce it.

Observation 2

Optimal effort rises with the breadth of the field, falls with AI capability.

Harder problems justify more effort. Better AI steals some of that justification.

Convictional
III · Dynamics · 16/24

Threshold one

Whether collapse is possible at all depends on α.

High α · inelastic effort

Knowledge does not collapse.

Even when general knowledge is low, people keep working. Regulated industries, protected professions, anything with high switching costs.

Low α · elastic effort

Knowledge can collapse.

When general knowledge is low enough, people stop. Competitive markets, optional work, anywhere the math has to pencil out period by period.

The counter-intuitive part

The steep, expensive cost curve (high α) is the safe regime. With a flat curve, marginal cost barely changes with effort — so any drop in general knowledge causes a large drop in effort. With a steep curve, even small changes in effort carry large cost consequences — so people hold their effort steady even when conditions deteriorate.

Lemma 2 — there is a critical α above which the system is stable regardless of starting point.

IV · Implications · 19/24

Read-out

Collapse is a coordination failure on top of individual rationality.

  • As AI gets better, the minimum richness of general knowledge needed to sustain engagement goes up.
  • Growing general knowledge attracts effort — a virtuous cycle. Shrinking general knowledge repels effort — a vicious one.
  • At the individual level, using AI more is always rational. The collapse, when it happens, is a thousand sensible individual choices adding up to a collective deficit.
Hold this one

Is the path to collapse inevitable if we just follow individual incentives? Or does it need a coordination failure stacked on top?

Convictional
IV · Implications · 20/24
Relax assumption 01

Private effort has some value without general knowledge.

In the model

Payoff from private effort = 0 when general knowledge = 0.

Relaxed

Private effort still returns something — and still emits some public signal.

⇒

A complete collapse can't happen. Strong AI slows the common signal but can't take it to zero. Knowledge degrades and stabilises at a lower level.

Convictional
IV · Implications · 21/24
Relax assumption 02

AI can contribute to the general signal.

In the model

AI adds nothing to the common pool.

Relaxed

AI contributes directly to general knowledge — at a rate that can outpace human withdrawal.

⇒

Not collapse. Substitution. Human-generated general knowledge is replaced by AI-generated general knowledge.

The question this opens

Whether that's good or bad depends entirely on whether AI-generated general knowledge is as good as human-generated, for the purposes of the field.

V · Discussion · 23/24

Open floor

Three questions.

  1. What does this imply for how we build Convictional? What are we already doing well — and what should we be thinking about differently?
  2. If a majority of the posts, decisions, and docs at Convictional were written or strongly shaped by AI — and the AI was good — is that a problem?
  3. Does private learning in the absence of general knowledge produce any signal or value? The paper says no. Is the paper right?
